# Style Checklist

Use this list after drafting or revising any passage. Every item should pass.

## Perspective
- [ ] Narrator never reports facts they could not know at that moment
- [ ] No sudden omniscient explanations
- [ ] Understanding is shown as partial, delayed, or revised later

## Information Reveal
- [ ] Major facts arrive in fragments, not complete packages
- [ ] Setting and background emerge through concrete observation
- [ ] The reader is allowed to connect dots the narrator has not yet connected

## Language
- [ ] Vocabulary stays plain and precise
- [ ] No pile-up of emotive adjectives
- [ ] Emotions are enacted (gesture, silence, redirected attention) rather than named

## Detail
- [ ] At least some sensory details exist that do not advance the immediate plot
- [ ] Objects, sounds, textures, and small actions receive quiet attention
- [ ] Repetition of ordinary phrases feels natural to the narrator’s voice

## Dialogue and Interaction
- [ ] Characters rarely explain themselves fully
- [ ] Subtext is present; the narrator may miss or only half-register it
- [ ] Silence, interruption, and incomplete sentences are allowed

## Overall Effect
- [ ] The passage can be read in a calm, almost flat tone without loss of power
- [ ] Nothing feels like an author briefing the reader
- [ ] A residual sense of something not fully grasped remains
