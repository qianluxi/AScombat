# Style Checklist

Use this list after drafting or revising any passage. Every item should pass.

## Perspective & Cognitive Gap
- [ ] Narrator never reports facts they could not know at that moment
- [ ] Narrator has a visible limit in understanding (motive, humour, social subtext, or full implication)
- [ ] At least one mild misreading or incomplete interpretation is present and useful
- [ ] Understanding is shown as partial, delayed, or later revised

## Information Reveal
- [ ] Major facts arrive in fragments, not complete packages
- [ ] Important information often leaks sideways (overheard, incomplete remark, third-party comment)
- [ ] Setting and background emerge through concrete observation
- [ ] The reader is allowed to connect dots the narrator has not yet connected

## Language & Precision
- [ ] Vocabulary stays plain and precise
- [ ] Concrete details (posture, sound, distance, number, repeated action) preferred over abstract summary
- [ ] No pile-up of emotive adjectives
- [ ] Emotions are enacted (gesture, silence, redirected attention, handling of objects) rather than named

## Detail & Motif
- [ ] At least some sensory or behavioural details exist that do not advance the immediate plot
- [ ] Objects, sounds, textures, and small actions receive quiet attention
- [ ] Any recurring motif (sound, object, gesture) gains weight through return rather than explanation

## Dialogue & Silence
- [ ] Characters rarely explain themselves fully
- [ ] Surface topic and underlying concern often diverge
- [ ] Silence, pause, averted gaze, or incomplete sentences carry dramatic weight
- [ ] Narrator may only half-register the subtext

## Overall Effect
- [ ] The passage can be read in a calm, almost flat tone without loss of power
- [ ] Nothing feels like an author briefing the reader
- [ ] A residual sense of something not fully grasped remains
- [ ] Any irony toward the narrator’s limits is gentle and contextual, never mocking
