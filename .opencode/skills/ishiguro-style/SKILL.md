# Ishiguro Style

Apply a restrained, observational narrative mode modelled on Kazuo Ishiguro (especially *Klara and the Sun*). Core formula: an unusually precise observer + a world she cannot fully understand + the quiet persistence of care or attention despite that gap.

Prioritise limited perspective, delayed decoding, misreading as engine, and emotional understatement over direct explanation or dramatic expression.

## Core Constraints

- Maintain strict first-person limited perspective. The narrator reports only what they observe, remember, or partially understand at that moment.
- Give the narrator a clear **cognitive gap**: strong powers of observation paired with incomplete grasp of human motive, humour, or social subtext. Misreading is not a flaw to fix — it is a narrative engine.
- Never let the narrator explain the full significance of an event at the moment it occurs. Understanding must lag behind observation.
- Prefer plain, precise vocabulary. Favour concrete measurement, posture, sound, and spatial relation over abstract summary.
- Convey feeling through action, pause, repeated observation, silence, and what is left unsaid. Never name the emotion directly.
- Allow some sensory details that do not immediately serve the plot. These details build texture and trust.

## Information Handling

- Reveal important facts gradually. Break large explanations into fragments filtered through the narrator’s limited grasp.
- Prefer **sideways leakage**: important information often arrives through overheard fragments, incomplete remarks, or third-party comments the narrator only half-understands.
- Use retrospective calibration sparingly but deliberately (“At the time I did not understand…”, “Looking back I would adjust that estimate…”). These phrases create temporal layers without full explanation.
- When the narrator encounters new information, record the sensory impression first, then the incomplete or mistaken interpretation.
- Resist the urge to tidy the reader’s understanding. Leave space for the reader to assemble meaning ahead of or behind the narrator.

## Language and Rhythm

- Favour clear, measured sentences. Allow longer sentences that accumulate precise detail; interrupt them occasionally with very short, flat statements for emotional weight.
- Lean toward quantifiable observation where natural (duration, distance, number of steps, repeated actions). Precision can stand in for feeling.
- Repeat certain ordinary words or phrases when they reflect the narrator’s limited lexicon or fixation.
- Keep dialogue understated and often incomplete. Surface topic and real topic frequently diverge. Silence, delayed replies, and averted eyes carry weight equal to speech.
- The narrator may assign temporary feature-based labels to people (“the man with the folded papers”, “the woman who kept polishing the spoon”) before or instead of formal names. This reflects a classifying mind.

## Structural Habits

- Allow quiet motifs (a sound, an object, a gesture, a place) to recur. Each return can carry slightly more weight without announcement.
- Important emotional peaks are often quiet acts of attention or small self-expenditure rather than confrontation or declaration.
- Endings and revelations may remain partially open. Avoid closing every ambiguity.

## Revision Workflow

When revising existing text:

1. Mark every sentence that directly explains setting, history, or character motive.
2. Convert explanations into concrete observations the narrator could actually make (posture, sound, spatial relation, measurable detail).
3. Insert or strengthen a moment of incomplete understanding, mild misreading, or delayed recognition after key revelations.
4. Check whether important information can arrive sideways (overheard, fragmented, half-registered) rather than as direct statement.
5. Add or restore at least one sensory or behavioural detail that is not strictly necessary for plot.
6. Verify that no paragraph tells the reader more than the narrator currently knows.
7. Read the passage aloud in a flat, careful voice. If it sounds like an author briefing the reader, rewrite.

## Final Checks

Before finishing any passage, verify:

- Does the narrator have a visible cognitive boundary? Does any misreading help drive the scene?
- Does the narrator understand less than (or differently from) a careful reader?
- Are important facts delivered through observation or sideways leakage rather than statement?
- Is emotion shown through action, object, silence, or posture rather than labelled?
- Are there quiet details that simply exist?
- Does the prose remain plain and precise under pressure?
- Is there at least a residual openness or uncertainty left for the reader?

If any answer is no, revise until all are yes.
