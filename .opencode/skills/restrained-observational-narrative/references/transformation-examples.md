# Transformation Examples

These examples show the shift from direct, explanatory prose to restrained observational prose. They are constructed for illustration and do not quote any copyrighted text.

## Example 1 — Setting Introduction

**Before (direct):**  
The academy was divided into five main buildings belonging to the Fire, Water, Wind, and Light departments, plus a separate Mechanics wing. Students from the Fire Alliance wore red collars; those from the Water Alliance wore blue.

**After (observational):**  
The roofs stretched in layers of dull grey along the lake. I counted five large buildings from the gate, though I did not yet know what each was for. Some students had a narrow band of dark red at the collar. Others had blue. At the time I thought it was only a matter of which subject they studied.

## Example 2 — Emotional Reaction

**Before (labelled):**  
I was shocked and deeply unsettled when I heard my father’s name spoken in connection with the disaster. Anger and confusion rose in me at once.

**After (shown):**  
The name was read out clearly. For a moment the classroom continued as before. Then I noticed that my hands had become very still on the desk. I looked at them as if they belonged to someone else. The rest of the lesson passed without my following it.

## Example 3 — Character Insight

**Before (explanatory):**  
Maren was precise and slightly disdainful of Fire Alliance habits. She clearly believed their way of working was careless.

**After (filtered):**  
She handed me the tools in a careful order, each one placed so that the next would be easy to reach. When my hand slipped on the nut she said nothing. She simply took it, turned it the correct way, and set it back. Later she asked whether people where I came from always preferred to finish quickly. I was not sure how to answer.

## Example 4 — Foreshadowing Detail

**Before (signposted):**  
I heard a strange high sound from the old machine in the corner. It would later prove important, but at the time I ignored it.

**After (quiet):**  
A thin, high note came from somewhere near the wall, like a finger drawn slowly around the rim of a glass. It lasted only a moment. I thought it was the sound of dishes and did not look up.

## Example 5 — Delayed Understanding

**Before (immediate):**  
Mother’s warning meant I must never speak of the mine disaster that had involved our family.

**After (partial):**  
That morning she had taken a long time tying the straps of my bag. When she finished she said only, “When you get there, don’t mention the mine.” She used the words we always used at home. I nodded, though I was not certain what shape the prohibition was meant to take.
