# Ishiguro Style

Apply a restrained, observational narrative mode modelled on Kazuo Ishiguro (especially *Klara and the Sun*). Prioritise limited perspective, delayed decoding, and emotional understatement over direct explanation or dramatic expression.

## Core Constraints

- Maintain strict first-person limited perspective. The narrator reports only what they observe, remember, or partially understand at that moment.
- Never let the narrator explain the full significance of an event at the moment it occurs. Understanding must lag behind observation.
- Prefer plain, precise vocabulary. Avoid ornate adjectives, rhetorical flourish, and explicit emotional labelling.
- Convey feeling through action, pause, repeated observation, and what is left unsaid.
- Allow some sensory details that do not immediately serve the plot. These details build texture and trust.

## Information Handling

- Reveal important facts gradually. Break large explanations into fragments filtered through the narrator’s limited grasp.
- Use retrospective framing sparingly (“I later understood…”, “At the time I did not notice…”). Prefer showing the gap rather than announcing it.
- When the narrator encounters new information, record the sensory impression first, then the incomplete interpretation.
- Resist the urge to tidy the reader’s understanding. Leave space for the reader to assemble meaning ahead of or behind the narrator.

## Language and Rhythm

- Favour clear, measured sentences. Mix longer observational sentences with shorter ones for pacing.
- Repeat certain ordinary words or phrases when they reflect the narrator’s limited lexicon or fixation.
- Avoid naming emotions directly (“I felt devastated”). Instead show the body, the interruption of routine, or a redirected attention.
- Keep dialogue understated. Characters often speak around the point; the narrator may misread or only partially register the subtext.

## Revision Workflow

When revising existing text:

1. Mark every sentence that directly explains setting, history, or character motive.
2. Convert explanations into concrete observations the narrator could actually make.
3. Insert a moment of incomplete or delayed understanding after key revelations.
4. Add or restore at least one sensory detail that is not strictly necessary for plot.
5. Check that no paragraph tells the reader more than the narrator currently knows.
6. Read the passage aloud in a flat, careful voice. If it sounds like an author briefing the reader, rewrite.

## Final Checks

Before finishing any passage, verify:

- Does the narrator understand less than (or differently from) a careful reader?
- Are important facts delivered through observation rather than statement?
- Is emotion shown rather than labelled?
- Are there quiet details that simply exist?
- Does the prose remain plain and precise under pressure?

If any answer is no, revise until all are yes.
