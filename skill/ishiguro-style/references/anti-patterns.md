# Anti-Patterns

Common ways prose drifts away from the target style, and how to correct them.

## 1. Author Briefing
**Symptom:** A paragraph that exists mainly to inform the reader of facts the narrator has no urgent reason to state.  
**Correction:** Convert the facts into something the narrator notices, misinterprets, or only partly registers. Move full explanation later or omit it.

## 2. Immediate Emotional Labelling
**Symptom:** “I felt a surge of anger / grief / confusion.”  
**Correction:** Show the physical or behavioural consequence instead. Let the reader name the feeling if they wish.

## 3. Complete Package Revelation
**Symptom:** A single speech or paragraph that delivers the entire background of an event, institution, or relationship.  
**Correction:** Break the information into at least two or three separate moments, each filtered through the narrator’s current limited understanding.

## 4. Over-Purposeful Detail
**Symptom:** Every sensory detail is clearly planted to serve a later plot point.  
**Correction:** Include a few details that simply exist — the weight of a cup, the way dust sits on a surface, the exact quality of a sound — without signalling their future importance.

## 5. Explanatory Dialogue
**Symptom:** Characters speak in ways that conveniently educate the reader or the protagonist.  
**Correction:** Let them speak incompletely, evasively, or about adjacent matters. The narrator may fail to grasp the full implication.

## 6. Premature Closure
**Symptom:** The narrator reaches a clear, settled understanding of an event within the same scene in which it occurs.  
**Correction:** Leave a residue of uncertainty. Allow later scenes to revise or complicate the first interpretation.

## 7. Ornamented Prose
**Symptom:** Sentences that prioritise elegance or emotional intensity over clarity of observation.  
**Correction:** Strip back to the precise thing seen, heard, or done. Trust the accumulation of plain statements.

## 8. Consistent Competence of Narrator
**Symptom:** The narrator always notices the “right” details and draws near-correct conclusions.  
**Correction:** Allow partial attention, mild misreading, and later correction. The narrator’s limits are part of the style.
